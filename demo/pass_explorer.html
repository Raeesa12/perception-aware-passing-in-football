How to read this
  • The shaded wedge is what the passer could see: 120 degrees around the direction he was moving.
  • Value says how much a pass to each teammate would raise his team's chances of scoring (and lower their chances of conceding) over the next few actions, compared with the pass he actually played. It comes from a model trained on thousands of real actions (VAEP), shown here multiplied by 100.
  • Green options were better, in view, onside and realistic to complete. Purple options were worth more but had a catch: out of view, offside, or a low chance of completing. Grey options were worth less than the pass played.
  • Teal ghosts show where the passer last saw a teammate who was out of view at the moment of the pass, as his memory would place him.
  • Play this pass sends the ball to that teammate at a typical pass speed while everyone else moves as they really did. A defender who could get to the ball first cuts it out. If it arrives, the receiver looks for the most dangerous pass he could make next. This is a simple projection, not a prediction of what would have happened.
  • Threat is the chance that a team in possession at that spot scores within the next few actions (expected threat).
  • The 64 World Cup 2022 matches are replayed from player tracking. Euro 2020 and Euro 2024 passes show the snapshot camera view at the moment of the pass, so nobody moves during a what-if.
